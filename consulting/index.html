The consulting version of Agent First

The founder should not have to architect the engagement.

A traditional agency asks you to choose from a menu: branding, web design, UX, development, analytics, marketing. That assumes you already know which discipline will solve the problem.

Agent First reverses that responsibility. You bring the goal, the constraints, your judgment and the decisions only you can make. The practice carries the research, remembering, organizing, translating, prioritizing, testing and figuring-out-what-comes-next burden.

The result is not seven disconnected vendors handing work to one another. It is one learning system moving from idea to evidence.

This is not “pick a service.”

It is: tell me what you are trying to accomplish. I determine which capabilities need to show up, in what order and what should be learned before we spend time or money on the next thing.

The operating belief

Technology should remove the burden around the work.

Consulting should too.

“Your job is not to know which expert to hire next. Your job is to know what you are trying to accomplish.”

Agent First began with a simple question about work: why are people still being asked to remember, search for, reconstruct and coordinate information that a well-designed system could carry for them?

The consulting practice applies that same question to founders and organizations. Why should the person with the vision also have to become the project manager, brand strategist, UX translator, analytics interpreter and traffic controller between every specialist?

Keep the human where humanity matters: direction, taste, relationships, judgment and accountability. Put the rest of the burden where it belongs.

Start with what you want to make happen

You do not need a scope of work. You need an outcome.

Tell me what you are building, fixing, launching or trying to understand.

You can be messy. You can be early. You can know exactly what is wrong without knowing what the solution is. Diagnosing the work is part of the work.
