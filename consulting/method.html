The Agent First Method

Traditional workforce design asks whether employees have been trained.

Agent First asks a different question: What does the environment require the employee to figure out in real time?

01

Context

Question: What does the employee need to know about this customer, account, client, product, location, or situation before any rule can be applied?

Failure mode: The worker starts searching before the system has resolved which context matters.

02

Rules

Question: Which policies, exceptions, constraints, permissions, and client-specific requirements govern the decision?

Failure mode: The “rule” is spread across notes, memory, training material, and tribal knowledge.

03

Retrieval

Question: How difficult is it to locate the right rule while the customer is waiting?

Failure mode: The employee is technically trained, but the correct information is too slow or cumbersome to retrieve.

04

Decision

Question: What judgment does the employee genuinely need to make, and what should already be resolved by the environment?

Failure mode: Routine interpretation is mistaken for empowerment, creating inconsistency and unnecessary cognitive load.

05

Communication

Question: What should the employee say, what wording is constrained, and how much adaptation is appropriate?

Failure mode: The worker finds the policy but still has to translate it into customer-ready language on the fly.

06

Action

Question: What happens next — schedule, transfer, escalate, document, explain, or stop?

Failure mode: Information is available, but the next operational step remains ambiguous.

07

Human Control

Question: What must remain visible, explainable, reviewable, and overridable by the worker or supervisor?

Failure mode: AI or automation becomes a black box instead of decision support.

Where this matters most

Rules-heavy, multi-client, exception-heavy work exposes the design problem fastest.

Multi-client contact centers

One workforce serves many clients whose rules can conflict even when the customer request sounds identical.

Insurance & regulated service

Policy interpretation, approved language, escalation, and documentation requirements create high decision density.

Healthcare administration

Eligibility, routing, exceptions, privacy constraints, and customer communication intersect in real time.

Property & field-service operations

Location-specific policies, service eligibility, vendor rules, and escalation paths often live in fragmented systems.
