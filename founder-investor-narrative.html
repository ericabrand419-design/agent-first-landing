Company narrative

Canonical Founder & Investor Narrative

One consistent story for how Agent First frames the problem, the design insight, the product, the initial wedge, the evidence strategy, the business model, the portfolio, and what would justify scale capital.

The problem

Rules-heavy frontline teams are often given access to information but are still expected to reconstruct the right context, policy, exception, wording, and next action while the customer is waiting.

More information ≠ better decisions

Knowledge bases, tabs, documents, training, and scripts can make information available while leaving interpretation and retrieval burden on the human.

Multi-client work compounds the burden

The same customer request may have a different correct answer by client, location, department, program, or contract.

The consequence

Search time, supervisor dependence, inconsistent promises, slower ramp, quality errors, and difficulty scaling complex accounts.

The insight

Stop forcing humans to reconstruct context before they can do their jobs. Keep the human in the customer relationship; move memory, context assembly, and approved rule application into the system.

Agent First is therefore not primarily a chatbot or a better folder. It is an operational decision layer behind the frontline worker.

The product

Agent First identifies the active client context, captures the current situation, applies approved client-specific rules, and surfaces what can happen, what must happen, what should be said/documented, and the next action.

Client-aware

Rules and guidance change with the represented account/program/location.

Rule-driven

Approved policy becomes operational states such as allow, verify, disclose, transfer, escalate, document, continue.

Human-first

The AI stays behind the agent. The human stays in front of the customer.

Initial wedge

Outsourced and multi-client contact centers create a concentrated version of the problem: one workforce represents many businesses with different rules. Automotive service is a proving ground because it exposes exceptions, hours, scheduling restrictions, advisor rules, service eligibility, transportation options, recalls, and escalation paths—but the intended product architecture is broader than automotive.

Business model and pilot strategy

Software

$69 per participating agent/month in the current pilot model, with recurring SaaS economics to be validated through real paid use.

Implementation

$2,500 standard Client Profile implementation fee; waived for accepted early pilot partners while the company is learning alongside them.

Conversion

$500 conversion credit for immediate continuation after the 90-day pilot.

The purpose of the paid pilot is not discounted consulting. It is to determine whether measurable value exists, whether the same core product can serve more than one customer, and whether buyers will continue paying.

Evidence strategy

Agent First does not need to claim traction it has not earned. The evidence progression is: product behavior → pilot hypothesis → raw observation → reviewed customer evidence → permissioned public evidence → repeated evidence across customers. The roadmap advances when uncertainty falls, not when feature count rises.

Potential moat

Configuration architecture

Ability to represent many client rule environments without customer-specific product forks.

Governed operational knowledge

Rule provenance, versioning, approval, testing, and translation from policy to frontline action.

Learning loop

Pilot/test evidence that improves configuration, onboarding, workflow design, and the commercial case without replacing customer-owned policy.

Portfolio

Agent First

Frontline decision-support product.

Agent First Consulting

Implementation/advisory/services line that can generate revenue and accelerate customer setup without confusing services with software.

Recallya

Separate relationship-intelligence/CRM product; internally, Agent First’s intended relationship operating system.

Capital story

Fundraising is not a milestone achieved by time passing. It becomes rational when capital can accelerate a motion that is already beginning to work: repeated customer value, commercial pull, understood implementation economics, clear product bottlenecks, and specific milestones that additional capital unlocks.
